# AI tells: patterns that make copy read machine-written

The Copy agent scans every line against this list and fixes each hit. The Ship agent also checks for em dashes in visible text.

This list is our own working summary, informed by Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" page from WikiProject AI Cleanup, plus contractor-site clichés we see constantly. The Wikipedia page itself warns that scrubbing these signs is not the real fix. The real fix is specifics: real towns, real jobs, the owner's own words. This file catches tells. The brief is what makes copy good.

**Refresh:** once a month, ask Claude Code to read the current Wikipedia page "Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing" and propose additions to this file. Joe approves every change. Never let the agent edit this file on its own.

---

## Puffed-up importance

Copy that inflates why something matters, in general terms.

- "stands as a testament to," "a testament to"
- "plays a vital role," "pivotal," "crucial," "underscores"
- "rich heritage," "enduring legacy," "deeply rooted"
- "nestled in," "boasts," "renowned"
- "commitment to excellence," "dedicated to quality"

**Fix:** say the actual thing. "Sam has roofed in Hilliard since 2011" beats "a testament to years of dedication."

## Contractor clichés

Every trades site says these. They signal nothing.

- "quality craftsmanship," "superior workmanship"
- "no job too big or too small"
- "one-stop shop," "full-service solution"
- "customer satisfaction is our top priority"
- "your dream home," "transform your space"
- "competitive pricing," "affordable rates"
- "family-owned and operated" with no detail after it (fine if followed by who and since when)

**Fix:** replace with a fact from the brief, or cut it.

## Sentence shapes

- **Negative parallelism:** "It's not just X, it's Y." / "More than just a roofer." Rewrite as a plain statement.
- **Triplets by default:** lists of three adjectives or three parallel phrases ("fast, reliable, and affordable"). Use two, or four, or one strong one.
- **Trailing -ing clauses:** ", ensuring...", ", making it...", ", allowing you to...". Split into a new sentence or cut.
- **Section-ending summaries:** "In short," "Ultimately," "At the end of the day," "When it comes to." Cut them.
- **Stacked transitions:** "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "In addition." Cut them.
- **Rhetorical questions as openers:** "Looking for a reliable roofer?" Lead with the answer instead.

## Words to cut on sight

elevate, seamless, seamlessly, unlock, level up, transform, transformative, delve, robust, tailored, journey, landscape, holistic, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, top-notch, hassle-free, look no further, peace of mind

## Proof and attribution

- Vague attribution: "customers say," "many clients agree," "experts recommend." Use a real, owner-approved review or nothing.
- Invented numbers: "hundreds of happy customers," "years of experience" with no number. Use the real figure from the brief or cut it.

## Formatting

- No em dashes in visible text. Use a period, comma, or colon.
- No emoji as bullets or icons.
- Headlines in sentence case, not Title Case On Every Word.
- No bolded phrase at the start of every list item.
